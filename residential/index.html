Upfront Pricing

Honest, Upfront Pricing

Good water heater service starts with knowing exactly what you're dealing with. Fullerton Water Heater offers honest, upfront pricing for every home, covering everything from a failed heating element to a complete tank replacement. Every visit begins with a thorough assessment of your water heater, including its age, capacity, fuel type, venting, and the condition of the tank and valves, followed by a clear recommendation and a written price before any work starts.

A lot of water heater failures send small signals well before the tank gives out. Keep an eye out for hot water with a rusty tint, popping noises when the burner fires, hot water that smells like rotten eggs, or showers that go cold sooner than they used to. Behind those symptoms are usually a thick layer of sediment, a spent anode rod, or a failing dip tube. A quick inspection at the first sign often costs far less than mopping up after a ruptured tank.

We also help homeowners plan ahead. Most tank water heaters last 8 to 12 years, and a planned water heater replacement is nearly always less expensive and less stressful than an emergency swap with water on the floor. Our technicians know how local water conditions wear on tanks over time, so we can tell you honestly whether your heater has years left or is living on borrowed time.

Maintenance Plans

Tank Flushing and Tune-Ups That Pay Off

Routine maintenance is the most reliable way to keep hot water flowing year-round. Regular service prevents failures before they start, clears out sediment, and catches small faults while they're still cheap to fix. At Fullerton Water Heater, every maintenance visit is built around your specific heater, covering a full tank flush, an anode rod inspection, a test of the temperature and pressure relief valve, a look at the burner and flue on gas models, element and thermostat testing on electric units, and a temperature check at the tap.

We offer flexible maintenance options for homeowners, with annual service for most tanks and more frequent flushing where the water is hard. That's especially important in hard-water areas, where sediment insulates the tank bottom, forces the burner to run longer, and shortens the life of the tank. Each home is assessed individually, and the plan is tailored to your heater's age, your household's hot water needs, and your local water.

The savings add up quickly. A flushed tank transfers heat with less effort, a fresh anode rod can add years to the tank, and a tested relief valve is ready to do its job if pressure ever climbs too high. Next to the cost of replacing a tank early, regular maintenance is a bargain.

Proper Sizing

A Water Heater Sized for Your Actual Household

Proper sizing is one of the most overlooked parts of any install. Before recommending a new heater, Fullerton Water Heater works through the details that determine your household's peak demand: the number of people and bathrooms, back-to-back showers, when the laundry and dishwasher run, and how cold the water entering the house gets. That gives you a heater with a first-hour rating or GPM capacity to handle your morning rush without wasting energy on excess capacity.

We walk you through the options for every home, especially growing families with rising hot water needs. A 40-gallon tank that served a couple rarely holds out once kids are showering back to back, and a tankless water heater can deliver continuous hot water so long as it's sized for the flow. If you're weighing tank against tankless, or gas against a heat pump model, our technician will lay out the honest trade-offs: upfront cost, operating cost, gas line and venting requirements, electrical service, and space, so you can pick what fits your home and budget.

Common Problems

The Water Heater Repairs Local Homes Need Most

A cold tap where hot water should be, a burner that refuses to light, a rumbling, sediment-heavy tank, a weeping relief valve: each of these problems has its own cause and calls for a different repair. Fullerton Water Heater tracks down the exact fault before swapping any parts, whether it's an electric tank with a burned-out element, a gas heater with a failing thermocouple, a broken dip tube sending cold water straight to the tap, or a tankless unit throwing error codes.

Some problems are mostly a nuisance, while others pose real risks. A burned-out element simply leaves you with lukewarm water, but a relief valve that drips or discharges is warning you that temperature or pressure inside the tank is too high, and a thermostat set above 120°F can burn a child in seconds. If you ever smell gas near the heater, leave the house and call your gas utility from outside before you call us. We match the fix to the failure: elements and thermostats on electric tanks, thermocouples, flame sensors, and gas valves on gas units, expansion tanks where water pressure is spiking, and leak repair on drain valves, fittings, and relief lines.
