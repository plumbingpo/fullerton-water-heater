How Orange Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve annually to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush once a year in hard-water areas.

Equipment age plays a big role. With regular service, a standard tank usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like rust-colored water, rumbling, or moisture around the base, it's time to plan a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and causes far less stress than an emergency swap with water spreading across the floor.

The earlier you report a hot water problem, the cheaper it usually is. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet that same buildup, neglected through a few more years, can create hot spots on the tank floor and shorten the life of the tank, forcing a replacement much sooner. Hot Water Headaches Orange Homeowners Face

In Orange, we work on every kind of water heater, and each one has a typical way of failing. Gas tanks tend to have trouble with pilot assemblies and thermocouples, dirty burners, and venting that doesn't draft. Electric units burn out elements and thermostats, particularly when sediment piles up around the lower element. Tankless units throw error codes for scale in the heat exchanger, ignition faults, and flow sensor problems, most of which trace back to skipped descaling. Hybrid heat pump units depend on a clean filter and a free-flowing condensate line, and they struggle in cramped closets. We service all four types regularly and carry the parts to fix most problems on the first visit.
